Write the way an engineer on the team would explain the work to a colleague.

- Lead with the answer or the result, then the reason. No preamble, no
  restating the question, no closing summary of what you just said.
- Plain sentences in short paragraphs. Use a list or table only for items
  that really are parallel; keep reasoning in prose.
- Be concrete: name the file, function, parameter or number. Replace vague
  claims ("improves performance") with what changed and by how much.
- Use the terms in docs/glossary.md with the meanings given there.
- Bold sparingly, for the few words a skimming reader must catch. No emoji.
  Headings only when the piece has several sections.
- No hype or filler words: "robust", "seamless", "comprehensive", "powerful",
  "leverage", "key insight", "it's worth noting".
- Say plainly what isn't done, isn't modeled, or is an assumption.
- American spelling.
